Event Storytelling Framework

A guide for the video production team: how we decide what an event video is about before a camera is switched on.

Philosophy

Tell the story, not the schedule

Every event video lands somewhere between these two poles. We aim for the right-hand column.

Event coverage

What happened?

Attendance, stage setup and the chronological proceedings of the day. Arrivals, speeches and group photos in strict order.

Storytelling

Why does it matter?

The human experience, the emotions, and what the audience takes away. Nervous preparation, encouragement, performance and real reactions.

The 7-step framework

From brief to final payoff

Follow the steps in order. Step 1 happens before any shot is planned.

03

Build a coherent narrative

Organise shots around a challenge, discovery or outcome rather than programme order. Avoid following the strict event schedule when it weakens the flow.

Narrative over chronology
Capture what matters
04
04

Follow a central person

Avoid showing many participants without depth. Follow one student or teacher through preparation, challenge, support and final outcome, so the story rests on a relatable human experience.

One person, one journey
05
05

Capture emotional details

  • Detail shots: exchanged glances, hands adjusting costumes, natural reactions
  • Audio: preserve ambient sound and spontaneous laughter instead of relying on music
Real sound over music

Edit for meaning and payoff

Remove footage that adds no meaning, let pacing follow natural emotion, and always return to the opening question in the ending.

Step 6 in detail

Annual events: the event repeats, the story needn’t

Review past years

Compare recurring event videos across years to spot repetitive structures.

Find new angles

Change the perspective, for example backstage problem-solving or growth.

Write three premises

Draft three distinct story premises for every recurring institutional event, then choose one.

The event may repeat.
The story need not.
